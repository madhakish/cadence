Cadence programs a 4-week wave per lift and advances it on the work you actually completed — never on the calendar. It does the plate math for the bars and plates your gym really owns, and it keeps every rep on your device. No account, no backend, no streaks.

One tap from every screen

Plate math for the gym you're standing in.

Type a target in lb or kg. Cadence solves the loading from that gym's actual inventory — including a kg plate and an lb plate on the same side — shows the achieved total in both units, and warns you when the rack can't get within 2 lb of the number.

Reverse mode works the other way: tap in what somebody left on the bar and get the total. Every gym you add keeps its own bars and plate counts, so the answer changes when you travel.

What's in it

Built for the twenty seconds between sets.

Everything below is on-device and works offline. Nothing here needs a subscription, an account, or a network round trip.

Plate math, mixed units

Per-side loading from your gym's real plate inventory, kg and lb on the same side, achieved total in both units, and a warning when the rack can't reach the target.

Programs that wait for you

A 4-week microcycle per lift — Volume 5×5, Load 5×3, Peak 3×3, Deload 3×5 — with double progression and strength waves offset per lift. Each lift advances off completed work, so a missed week doesn't desync the plan.

Logging that survives edits

Pre-filled warmup ramps, add or remove single sets, propagate a weight change across the remaining planned work, and keep planned, completed and skipped visibly distinct. Editing reps never resets the weight.

Rest timer that follows the lift

5:00 after main work, 1:30 after accessories, overridable per exercise. On iOS it runs as a Live Activity on the Lock Screen, with a local notification when it's time to go again.

Body signals, not vibes

Optional shoulder, hip and knee flags on any set, a per-site timeline you can read months later, and an optional next-morning knee check-in after you run.

Your gym tag, at the front desk

Store a photo of the membership barcode per gym and show it full-screen from the launch screen — the iOS app takes the display to full brightness for you. Your phone becomes the second keychain tag the gym's software refuses to issue.

A training day

Open it in the doorway, close it in the car park.

The full walkthrough — including building a program from scratch — is in How to use it.

Pick a style, or build your own

Start from a pre-programmed style — novice linear progression, Texas Method, 5/3/1, conjugate — or lay out your own days and lifts. Templates are starting points, not commitments: everything stays editable at any time, mid-cycle included.

Open the day, then start it

Opening a session shows the prescription and lets you change it. The clock only starts when you say you've started, so browsing the plan in the changing room never invents a two-hour workout.

Log the sets you did

Warmups arrive pre-filled. Enter in kg or lb — it's stored canonically in pounds either way. Mark sets clean, grindy or wobbly if you want the engine to notice, and hit "Dropping load" when it isn't there today: Cadence uses a pre-computed fallback and records why.

Read the summary, then forget about it

Volume, top sets and auto-detected PRs — heaviest set, first time at a rep scheme, volume record. The next rotation is computed from what you performed, so there's nothing to reconcile before next time.

Where it runs

Two apps, one brain.

The training math lives in a shared, platform-free core that both clients mirror one-to-one, with test suites on each side that fail if the two ever disagree. Same programs, same numbers, platform-appropriate UI.

Beta

iOS 17+

The native app: SwiftUI and SwiftData, a Lock Screen Live Activity for the rest timer, Control Center controls for rest and your gym tag, and optional write-only HealthKit. Not on the App Store yet — TestFlight, or build it yourself.

Android

A native Android client is on the roadmap and will share the same core logic and the same portable backup file. Until it lands, the web app is a first-class Android experience — install it from Chrome's menu.

Use the web app

There is no server to leak

Cadence has no backend, no accounts and no analytics. Your log lives in the app's own on-device store; the web app never sends a request anywhere except to fetch its own files. Health data, when you enable it, is written to Apple Health and never read back.

Because there's no cloud copy, backups matter: Cadence exports one portable JSON file (plus CSV) that restores on either platform.

What's stored, exactly

Open source, and readable

The whole thing is on GitHub: the iOS app, the web app, the shared training core, the documented backup format, and the machine-checked registry of behavioural rules that aren't allowed to change silently.

Issues and pull requests are welcome. So is forking it and pointing it at your own training opinions.
