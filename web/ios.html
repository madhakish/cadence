Cadence on iPhone

The native app is real, builds on every commit, and ships to TestFlight automatically — but it isn't on the App Store yet. There are three honest ways to run it today, and one of them takes about two minutes.

Join the beta Build it yourself Or just use the web app

Route 1 — recommended

The TestFlight beta

TestFlight is Apple's own beta distribution system. Nothing is sideloaded, nothing is jailbroken, and updates arrive like normal App Store updates. Every release Cadence tags is uploaded to TestFlight by CI.

What you need

  • An iPhone or iPad on iOS 17 or later.
  • Apple's free TestFlight app from the App Store.
  • An Apple ID. No payment details, no developer account.

TestFlight builds expire 90 days after upload, so during an active beta you'll be updating every so often. TestFlight tells you when.

How to get in

Public beta enrolment isn't open yet — the first build has to clear Apple's beta review before a link can be handed out. Ask to be included and you'll get the link as soon as it exists.

Ask for beta access

That opens a pre-filled GitHub issue. Don't put your email address in it — a public TestFlight link doesn't need one, and a public issue is a bad place for personal data.

Route 2 — for anyone with a Mac

Build it yourself

It's open source, so you don't have to wait for me. A free Apple ID is enough to run it on your own device.

Steps

Requires macOS with Xcode 15 or later. The Xcode project is generated rather than committed, so generate it first:

git clone https://github.com/madhakish/cadence.git
cd cadence
brew install xcodegen
xcodegen generate
open Cadence.xcodeproj

In Xcode, select the Cadence scheme, set your own signing team on the app and widget targets, pick your iPhone, and run.

Worth knowing

  • With a free Apple ID the signature lasts 7 days — after that the app refuses to launch until you rebuild. A paid developer account extends that to a year.
  • You'll need to trust the developer profile once, under Settings → General → VPN & Device Management.
  • Change the bundle identifier to something of your own if you also have the TestFlight build installed.
  • The shared training logic has its own test suite: cd CadenceCore && swift test.

Route 3 — for the technically inclined

Builds attached to every release

Each tagged release carries two artifacts built by CI. Neither is a double-click install on an iPhone, so treat this as the route for people who already know what they're doing.

AssetWhat it's forWhat it needs
Cadence-<version>-simulator.app.zip Running the app in Xcode's iOS Simulator on a Mac — the quickest way to look at it without a device. A Mac with Xcode. Drag the unzipped .app onto a booted simulator.
Cadence-<version>-unsigned.ipa Installing on your own device with your own signature. Unsigned on purpose — you re-sign it with your own Apple ID before it will install. Same 7-day limit as a free-account build.

What the native app adds

The training logic is identical to the web app by design — the difference is what iOS lets it do outside the app.

Live Activity

The rest countdown lives on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island, with pause, resume and skip controls on the workout face — so an abandoned session's stopwatch can always be stopped without unlocking.

Action Button & Control Center

Start or skip rest without picking the phone up properly — via the Action Button, or (on iOS 18) a Control Center and Lock Screen control. Useful when your hands are chalked and the bar is loaded.

Gym Tag control

On iOS 18, a second Control Center and Lock Screen button goes straight to your membership barcode — one press between arriving at the door and being scanned in.

Apple Health, optionally

Off by default, and write-only when you turn it on: Cadence writes workouts and bodyweight and never reads anything back out of Health.

Local notifications

Rest-timer alerts and the optional next-morning knee check-in after you run. No push server involved — they're scheduled on the device.

Native storage

SwiftData on device, with tested migrations for every version that ever shipped. An upgrade is never allowed to require reinstalling and losing your log.

Being a beta tester

What I actually need from you

Cadence is used mid-workout, one-handed, in bad light, by someone who wants to get back under the bar. That's the thing I can't test alone: whether it stays out of the way when you're tired.

The most useful reports are the boring ones — a tap that needed two attempts, a number that looked wrong, an edit that disappeared, a prescription you didn't trust. Log a real session or two and tell me where it annoyed you.

Report something Open issues

House rules

  • Never paste your log into an issue. Workouts, bodyweight, health signals and gym membership details are exactly the data this project refuses to collect. Describe the shape of the problem instead.
  • Export before you update. Settings → Data → Export JSON. Betas are betas.
  • Say which build. The version number is in Settings, and TestFlight shows the build it installed.
  • Data safety is the priority bug class. Anything that loses or corrupts a logged set gets fixed before anything cosmetic.

Will it be on the App Store?

Maybe. It's a single-user logbook with no accounts and no server, which is a slightly awkward shape for the App Store, and TestFlight already distributes it properly. Until that changes, the beta plus the source is the honest answer.

What won't change: no subscription, no ads, no analytics, and no account. If it ever ships to the App Store it ships the same way it works now.

Android

A native Android client is planned. It will share the same platform-free training core and the same portable backup file, so a log started on the web app or on iOS will move over intact.

Until then the installed web app is a genuinely good Android experience — Chrome's Install app gives it a home-screen icon, full-screen launch and offline use.
