# Declare a training break

A gap you chose should never read as a lapse. When you take time off — or
train deliberately light — declare it, and Cadence reads the calendar the way
you meant it.

## Add a break

1. Open **Settings → Training breaks** and tap **Add break**.
2. Pick the kind:
   - **Deload** — planned reduced-load training inside a cycle. Sessions are
     still expected on deload days.
   - **Rest** — planned days off. Not missed days.
   - **Away** — travel, gym closure, illness, layoff. Not missed days, and
     Cadence will suggest easing back in when it ends.
   - **Active recovery** — real training, deliberately off-program. Sessions
     you bank during it are saved as history but never advance program
     progression or PR baselines, and the bank summary says so.
3. Enter it the way you think of it — a **number of days** from a start date,
   or an explicit **date range**. Both write the same inclusive day span, and
   editing reopens in the shape you used.
4. Add an optional note (where you were, what the plan was).

## What changes while a break is active

- Today's spacing advisory ("N days since your last session…") stays quiet
  when a rest, away, or active-recovery span covers the gap.
- The coach's shorter-spacing trial ignores gaps a declared break overlaps —
  a vacation is not evidence about your preferred training frequency.
- The History log shows the break as its own row among your sessions.
- Nothing about the program itself moves: cycle, rotation, and the next-day
  pointer are exactly where you left them, and deleting a break simply
  restores the ordinary reading of those days.

## Coming back

After an **away** break ends, Today shows a re-entry note for up to a week —
until you bank a session. The first session back is re-entry, not a test.

Breaks travel in your JSON backup (schema version 10), so a restore on either
platform keeps them.
